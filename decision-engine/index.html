From model scores to business decisions.

A decision simulator that separates three questions that are often collapsed into one: who is likely to respond, who is likely to respond because of the intervention, and where an intervention actually creates economic value.

THE PROBLEM

High propensity ≠ high incremental value.

A model can identify customers who are very likely to convert without an intervention. Targeting them may create little incremental impact while spending scarce incentive budget.

THE APPROACH

Predict → estimate incrementality → optimize.

The simulator turns model outputs into an explicit policy: intervene, hold out, or deprioritise — based on expected incremental value rather than propensity alone.

01 · DECISION FRAME

The prediction is not the decision.

For each customer, the simulator keeps the prediction, causal effect and economics separate.

01 · PROPENSITYP(Y=1 | X)How likely is the outcome under the observed population?
02 · INCREMENTALITYτ(X)How much does the intervention change the outcome for this customer?
03 · VALUEΔValue − CostDoes intervening create positive expected business value?
Core decision rule: intervene when expected incremental value × unit economics − intervention cost is positive, subject to risk and capacity constraints.

04 · METHOD

How the simulator is constructed.

This is a proof-of-work system, not a claim that synthetic estimates are production estimates.

1. Generate heterogeneous customers

Customers receive latent propensity and treatment-effect characteristics. This creates a population where high-propensity users are not necessarily the users with the largest treatment effect.

2. Produce model scores

A noisy propensity score is generated from the latent outcome propensity. Precision controls how much noise is introduced into ranking.

3. Estimate incremental value

The policy uses the simulated conditional treatment effect τ(X), multiplied by value per incremental conversion, less intervention cost.

4. Apply constraints

Capacity limits prevent the policy from targeting everyone with positive predicted value. The engine ranks candidates by expected net value and applies the operational constraint.

5. Stress-test the policy

Threshold, model quality, unit economics and capacity can be changed independently. This makes the business sensitivity visible instead of hiding it behind a single model metric.

6. Validate the decision layer

The key output is not AUC. It is incremental conversions, incremental value, cost, net value and ROI under a clearly stated targeting policy.

Important distinction: propensity modelling answers “who is likely to convert?” Uplift / causal modelling answers “who changes because of treatment?” The decision layer adds the economic question: “where should we spend the intervention?”
