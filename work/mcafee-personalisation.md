  <h1>Personalised product recommendations & customer segmentation</h1>
  <p class="case-lead">Using behavioural signals to improve product relevance, target retention interventions and support commercial decisions.</p>

  <h2>The problem</h2>
  <p>McAfee customers differed significantly in behaviour, engagement and product needs. A one-size-fits-all experience made it harder to identify which products and interventions were relevant to which customers.</p>
  <p>The broader objective was to use customer-level data to improve product relevance and support <b>subscription churn reduction</b>.</p>

  <h2>Customer segmentation</h2>
  <p>I developed customer segmentation approaches to identify behavioural patterns across customer groups. The goal was to move from aggregate customer behaviour to a more granular understanding of customer needs and engagement.</p>
  <p>These segments provided a foundation for personalised recommendation strategies and customer-value analysis.</p>

  <h2>Personalised recommendations</h2>
  <p>I developed personalised product recommendation models designed to improve product relevance for different customer segments. The work connected customer behaviour and segmentation to recommendation decisions rather than treating recommendations as an isolated modelling problem.</p>

  <h2>ML workflow</h2>
  <p>The modelling workflow used <b>MLflow</b>, giving the project a production-oriented model development and management workflow.</p>
  <div class="impact"><b>Recommendation Systems</b> · <b>Personalisation</b> · <b>MLflow</b> · customer segmentation</div>

  <h2>Uplift modelling for targeting</h2>
  <p>I also applied <b>uplift modelling</b> to customer targeting. Instead of predicting only who was likely to respond, the objective was to identify customers whose behaviour could be changed by a retention or engagement intervention.</p>
  <p>This distinction is important in customer targeting because customers who would respond without an intervention are different from customers whose response is attributable to the intervention itself.</p>

  <h2>Connecting ML to commercial decisions</h2>
  <p>I extended the customer-level analysis into dynamic subscription pricing by analysing segment-level behaviour and price sensitivity. This connected customer understanding with commercial decisions around conversion and pricing efficiency.</p>


  <h2>From prediction to intervention</h2>
  <p>A key part of the work was distinguishing between understanding customers and deciding what to do for them. Segmentation established behavioural groups; recommendation models translated those patterns into product suggestions; uplift modelling added an intervention lens for retention and engagement targeting.</p>
  <p>This created a more decision-oriented ML workflow: identify the customer context, estimate what action is relevant, and connect the output to a commercial or customer-experience objective.</p>

  <h2>What this demonstrates</h2>
  <ul>
    <li><b>Data Science / ML:</b> segmentation, recommendation systems and uplift modelling.</li>
    <li><b>Product thinking:</b> connecting models to customer experience and retention objectives.</li>
    <li><b>Applied ML:</b> using MLflow within the model development workflow.</li>
    <li><b>Commercial thinking:</b> extending customer-level modelling into pricing and conversion decisions.</li>
  </ul>
