  <h1>Cost-to-Serve</h1>
  <p class="case-lead">Combining predictive analytics, process analysis and business trade-offs to reduce customer servicing costs.</p>

  <h2>The problem</h2>
  <p>Customer servicing costs were influenced by customer characteristics, case complexity, agent workload, resolution behaviour and operational processes. The challenge was to identify the drivers that mattered and support decisions that balanced customer outcomes with operational cost.</p>

  <h2>Predictive analytics</h2>
  <p>I developed a <b>Case Resolution Time prediction model</b> incorporating customer, agent and workload characteristics. The objective was to identify operational bottlenecks and prioritise high-impact case types.</p>

  <h2>Cost-to-Serve decision support</h2>
  <p>As part of the Cost-to-Serve initiative with the Dispute Analysis team, I developed sensitivity analyses around provisional refund amounts to evaluate trade-offs between customer outcomes and operational costs.</p>

  <h2>Process analysis</h2>
  <p>I applied process mining to complaint-resolution journeys to identify inefficiencies across stages and worked with stakeholders to identify root causes and targeted process improvements.</p>

  <h2>Outcome</h2>
  <div class="impact"><b>£124 → £87</b> annual Customer Serving Cost per customer</div>
  <p>I also automated customer campaign tracking through a CXO-facing dashboard, replacing manual reporting and improving operational visibility, with efficiency equivalent to <b>0.5 FTE</b>.</p>


  <h2>From model to operational decision</h2>
  <p>The predictive model was one component of a broader decision-science workflow. Customer, agent and workload characteristics were used to understand case-resolution time; sensitivity analysis explored operational trade-offs; and process mining examined where complaint-resolution journeys were creating inefficiency.</p>
  <p>The work therefore combined modelling with process understanding and stakeholder decision support, rather than optimising a model in isolation.</p>


  <h2>Breaking down the operational problem</h2>
  <p>Cost-to-Serve was not a single-model problem. Customer servicing cost reflected a combination of case complexity, customer characteristics, agent workload and the sequence of actions required to resolve a complaint. I therefore approached it through complementary analytical lenses.</p>

  <h2>Predicting resolution effort</h2>
  <p>The Case Resolution Time model used <b>customer, agent and workload characteristics</b> to estimate resolution time. This created a quantitative view of where servicing effort was being consumed and which case characteristics were associated with longer resolution journeys.</p>

  <h2>Testing business trade-offs</h2>
  <p>I used sensitivity analysis around <b>provisional refund amounts</b> to understand the relationship between customer outcomes and operational cost. This helped shift the discussion from an isolated operational metric toward explicit trade-offs that business stakeholders could evaluate.</p>

  <h2>Understanding the process</h2>
  <p>Predictive modelling alone could identify where effort was concentrated, but not necessarily why. I complemented the model with <b>process mining of complaint-resolution journeys</b> to identify inefficiencies across the process and support root-cause analysis.</p>

  <h2>Turning analysis into an operating tool</h2>
  <p>The work also included automating customer campaign tracking through a <b>CXO-facing dashboard</b>, replacing manual reporting and making the output more usable for ongoing business monitoring.</p>

  <h2>Outcome</h2>
  <p>The Cost-to-Serve work contributed to reducing annual Customer Serving Cost per customer from <b>£124 to £87</b>. The dashboard automation delivered efficiency equivalent to <b>0.5 FTE</b>.</p>

  <h2>What this demonstrates</h2>
  <ul>

    <li><b>FDE / Solutions Engineering:</b> combining data, analysis and stakeholder requirements into practical operational solutions.</li>
    <li><b>Business impact:</b> connecting technical analysis to cost and process outcomes.</li>
  </ul>
